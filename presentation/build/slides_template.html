The choice is hard, high-stakes and uncertain

  • Resection can stop seizures, but only if the right tissue is found, and it carries risk (memory decline in 36% after temporal surgery in one RCT).
  • Neurostimulation (RNS, DBS, VNS) is safer but palliative: roughly 40–75% fewer seizures, seizure freedom uncommon.
  • No study reports outcomes after model-guided stimulation.
  • You cannot try both on the same patient. A virtual-brain twin could, if its answers can be trusted.

ScienceClaw Epilepsy Collective · research evidence on virtual patients, not clinical recommendations
One in three people with epilepsy keep having seizures despite medication. For them the choice is surgery, which can cure but only if we find the right tissue, or neurostimulation, which is safer but rarely stops seizures. Nobody can test both on the same patient. We asked: can a collective of AI agents build and test virtual-brain twins for this choice, rigorously enough that we would trust what it says, and what it refuses to say?
Our system

Agents propose, a deterministic controller decides

Distributed by design

  • Roles hold different information and powers: only R1 browses, only R3 uses the cloud VM, only R5 can open the sealed vault.
  • A result counts only after a different role gates it. The producer cannot approve its own work.
  • Agents post unmet needs any eligible role can claim; failed gates trigger repairs that mark downstream work stale.
  • The trajectory adapts to evidence: in study-001 the plan stopped at the model gate.

Honest limit: LEAD proposes tasks, so this is governed, not leaderless. The controller, not LEAD, decides what is admitted and accepted.

ScienceClaw Epilepsy Collective · built on the ScienceClaw framework plan
This is our system. Seven roles, each a Claude agent with its own skill, tools and permissions: R1 searches and verifies literature, R2 curates real patient recordings, R3 builds the virtual patient, R4 designs resections and stimulation, R5 independently re-checks everything, RV reviews clinically, and a lead proposes work. Agents do not decide what counts. A deterministic controller does: it admits or refuses each task against the role's profile, validates every artifact, and accepts a gate only from a role other than the producer. Everything goes into an append-only ledger. Ground truth is sealed in a vault only the evaluator can open, and skills are frozen when a study starts. Agents post unmet needs that others claim.

studies/study-001/report.md · reports/study-001-summary.html · R5 verdict A0098
Study-001 ran on real data in six hours. It produced a literature dossier of 32 source-checked claims: resection gives the best chance of seizure freedom when there is a resectable focus, and stimulation reduces seizures but rarely stops them. It curated 10 real intracranial-EEG patients from OpenNeuro. And when R3 fitted the published virtual-brain pipeline, R5 showed it was not qualified: no convergence, the planted focus not recovered, four code defects. So the system stopped: no simulations, no predictions, and the outcome vault stayed sealed.

Study-002 · virtual treatment results  exploratory · model not qualified

In the model, ablation beat stimulation

Caveats (from the record)

  • Model not qualified; fits are a MAP ensemble, not a posterior.
  • Primary window: no baseline seizures in any of 24 fits, so no ranking. Shown: sensitivity window, 12 fits.
  • Simulated baseline did not match the recording (0/10 top channels).
  • 4 high-amplitude pulse-train arms failed numerically; excluded, not dropped.
  • The ablated set is 9 regions in both hemispheres: an in-silico test, not a feasible operation.
  • Not yet compared with the sealed truth.
A0038-SimulationBatch (R4, T0017): payload.json, full_runs.json, full_signals_median_sample.npz
The numbers, exploratory. In the sensitivity window, twelve model fits seize in a median of 3.5 regions. Ablating the estimated zone plus the propagation zone cut that by a median of 3 and abolished all seizures in 5 of 12 fits. The SEEG pulse-train stimulation changed nothing; sustained current helped a little and never abolished seizures. But, and the agents wrote these caveats themselves: the model is not qualified, the primary window had no seizures at all, the simulated baseline does not match the recording, and nothing has been checked against the sealed truth yet.

Why it can help patients

  • Compare a personalised resection with stimulation before any operation.
  • Guardrails make a "no" visible: blinding, gates by another role, frozen releases, provenance for every number.
  • Study-001 shows the system says "not qualified" instead of overclaiming.
